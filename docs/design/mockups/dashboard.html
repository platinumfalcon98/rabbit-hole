    Fits inside a VS Code webview

    • One bundled OFL font (Martian Mono, ~90 KB woff2) loaded through the existing font-src ${cspSource}. No network.
    • Every chart is text or plain DOM. Chart.js can be dropped entirely, which shrinks the webview bundle and kills the canvas glow workaround.
    • The fieldset/legend box titles and block glyphs (█▓▒░) render identically in Electron on every OS.
    • The CRT is four fixed layers over the webview. The phosphor mask is a <canvas> pattern blended with multiply and redrawn only on resize; a canvas needs no CSP change, where a mask image would need img-src data:. Scanlines, the refresh roll and the glass are CSS gradients.
    • The mask darkens what it covers, so the page gets the brightness back through filter: brightness(). Mask and strength would be one setting, with “off” one click away.
    • Ranges swap the day tape for one segmented column per day (green: target met) and keep the tape underneath as an average active day, so when you work survives the aggregation. Click a column to open that day. Lines go per day up to two weeks, per week beyond; sessions become one line per day.
    • Hover a project or a language (a legend key, a segment of a day's bar, a language row) and every other panel narrows to it: the columns, the day tape, lines, languages and files. The panel under the pointer keeps its rows and dims the rest, and panels hold their height, so nothing moves while you look. On the activity tab, hovering a project row does the same to the year and the heatmap.
    • The days card scrolls on its own. That is plain overflow-y: auto, which a VS Code webview handles like any page, with a scrollbar drawn in the terminal's colours.
    • Light themes get a paper terminal with a faint mask; dark themes get the full tube. High contrast and forced-colors remove every layer; reduced motion stops the roll and flicker.

    All numbers are example data shaped like a real day on this repo.
